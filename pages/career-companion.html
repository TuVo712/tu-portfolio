01 / The challenge

Preparing a CV and finding relevant opportunities can be overwhelming, especially when application requirements, language preferences and unfamiliar recruitment terminology add extra barriers.

Design question: How might we make CV preparation and job discovery more approachable without taking control away from the applicant?

For our hackathon concept, the team focused on the CV-building and application-discovery stage of the career journey.

02 / Our approach

We explored a career-support companion that could help users organise information they already have, improve how their experience is presented and discover jobs aligned with their skills.

The concept combines bilingual CV creation, user-reviewed suggestions and job matching in a single, accessible experience. It was designed as a prototype rather than a deployed recruitment platform.

♡Accessible by design

Clear labels, readable layouts and keyboard-friendly interactions.

✎User stays in control

Review, edit or reject proposed CV changes before saving.

✳Grounded in real experience

Reorganise supplied information without inventing qualifications.

03 / From CV to opportunity

We mapped a flexible journey that accommodates users starting with an existing CV or creating one from scratch.

  1. Start with a CVUpload an existing document or build a new CV using guided fields.
  2. Review suggestionsSee proposed improvements and choose whether to accept, reject or edit them.
  3. Confirm and exportApprove the information to keep, then preview an ATS-friendly CV in English or Vietnamese.
  4. Explore opportunitiesCompare skills with job requirements and inspect potential matches, including pasted job listings.

Optional notifications for future matching opportunities were part of the wider product vision, not evidence of a working live alert service.

04 / Decisions that mattered

✓ Consent before saving

Only user-approved information should be retained.

↔ English + Vietnamese

Support CV preparation across both languages without changing the applicant's facts.

◎ ATS-friendly output

Use clear headings and a logical reading order for machine-readable exports.

Accessibility considerations included WCAG 2.2 guidance, visible control labels, keyboard operation, appropriate status announcements and avoiding overly crowded screens. These were design targets, not a claim of independently verified WCAG compliance.

AI boundary: Suggested edits should only restructure or clarify user-provided details. The assistant must not invent skills, qualifications or work history, and must not alter a user's photo.

06 / What I learned

This project reinforced how much product design depends on deciding what an AI assistant shouldn't do. Giving users a way to review suggested changes is just as important as generating suggestions in the first place.

A production version would need secure consent-based data handling, reliable job-source integrations, bilingual quality checks, ATS export validation and usability testing with diverse applicants. The prototype is a starting point for those questions, not a finished job-search service.
