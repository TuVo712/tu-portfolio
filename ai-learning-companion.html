PROJECT CASE STUDY / UX + ACCESSIBILITY

Learning, with a little help. ✳

Designing a course-aware AI companion to help learners understand difficult concepts while staying connected to the lesson.

UX researchAccessible learningInteraction designInteractive prototype
Focus

Supporting learners with ADHD in online learning

My contribution

Prototype development, interaction logic and design refinements

Deliverable

Interactive, predefined-response prototype

01 / The challenge

When learners encounter unfamiliar terminology or difficult concepts in online lessons, looking elsewhere for answers can interrupt their flow. For some learners with ADHD, switching tabs or following tangential explanations may make returning to the original lesson harder.

Design question: How might we offer helpful explanations at the moment of confusion without turning the companion into another distraction?

02 / Research-informed approach

The team drew on research literature to identify barriers and challenge assumptions about how AI assistance should work. We developed and refined the concept through a research-informed co-design process, then gathered feedback from learners with ADHD on the prototype.

Rather than assuming that more AI assistance is always better, the design aims to keep learners in control and encourage them to return to the learning material.

03 / Designing the support

The companion offers three different starting points, depending on what the learner needs.

✦ Explain it

Get a contextual explanation of a concept.

💡 Give me a hint

Receive a nudge instead of a complete answer.

✓ Check my understanding

Move from reading explanations to actively reflecting on learning.

Two additional interaction rules help prevent endless exploration: after three consecutive interactions, the companion suggests an understanding check; after four consecutive interactions, it reminds the learner to return to the lesson.

04 / What we prototyped

The interactive prototype demonstrates how a course-aware assistant could sit alongside lesson content. It includes a draggable, resizable companion panel, contextual terminology support, hints, explanations and understanding checks.

Prototype boundary: Responses, contextual content and interaction flows are predefined. The prototype demonstrates the intended user experience; it is not yet a fully autonomous AI agent or a live retrieval system.

A full implementation would require reliable retrieval of approved course content, handling of natural-language questions, safeguards against unsupported answers and further accessibility and usability testing.

05 / Feedback and refinements

Learner feedback helped clarify the importance of concise explanations, staying focused on the lesson and avoiding over-reliance on AI. One concern was that instantly explaining everything might discourage independent thinking.

We used the feedback to refine the assistance flow, including hint-first support and prompts that encourage active understanding. Due to time constraints, these refinements were not put through a second round of participant testing.

06 / What I learned

This project reinforced that a helpful AI experience isn't necessarily one that answers every question immediately. Designing support also means deciding when to offer less help, when to check understanding and when to guide someone back to their original task.

My next steps would be to test the revised interactions with learners, evaluate whether the reminders feel supportive rather than disruptive, and explore how a full system could ground its answers in verified course materials.
